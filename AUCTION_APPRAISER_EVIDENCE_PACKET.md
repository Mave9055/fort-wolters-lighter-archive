## 2. Neutral Identification

1965 **PARK Lighter / Precision Made by Park Industries, Murfreesboro, Tennessee** slim presentation lighter inscribed to **WOC Arthur P. Lancy Jr.** The obverse bears **Class 65-9WA** and the **Hughes World Leader** helicopter insignia. The reverse inscription states that Lancy was a member of the **first graduating class using the Hughes TH-55A helicopter** at the U.S. Army Primary Helicopter Training School, Fort Wolters, Texas, 1965. The artifact is presented in its **as-found, unserviced** state: never cleaned, fueled, or operated for spark. No claim is made that the lighter was carried in Vietnam, used in combat, or officially issued by Hughes or the U.S. Army beyond what is visibly engraved or stamped on the object.

## 8. Class & Roster Evidence

### Historical placement

Class **65-9W** represents a pivotal, historically documented moment in Army Aviation. In **1964**, the Army formally transitioned primary flight training from fixed-wing aircraft to the **Hughes TH-55A Osage**. Class **65-9W** was among the **very first full graduating classes** to complete the new rotary-wing pipeline.

### The five-point rarity argument

1. **First TH-55A graduating-class context:** The lighter’s reverse inscription explicitly references membership in the **first graduating class using the Hughes TH-55A helicopter** — a cohort tied to the Army’s 1964 shift to rotary-wing primary training at Fort Wolters.
2. **Independent cross-verification:** The handwritten roster lists **“Lancy”** in the top row, and the period-engraved lighter names **WOC Arthur P. Lancy Jr.** — two independent primary sources pointing to the same person, same class, same historical moment.
3. **Class attrition documentation:** The notation **“(orig. class mem.)”** associated with the front-row entries (**Jewell, Breton, and Neal** on the transcribed roster) documents that class composition changed mid-training — recording washouts, transfers, or replacements. This granular human detail is typically lost to time and is absent from official U.S. Army forms.
4. **Vietnam-era cohort significance:** These 1965 Fort Wolters WOCs entered one of the highest-demand aviation career fields of the Vietnam War era. A documented roster of who trained together on the TH-55A before later deployment is precisely the type of primary material aviation historians and families seek. *(This describes historical cohort context; it does not prove this lighter was carried in Vietnam or used in combat.)*
5. **Matched Set premium:** The VHPA archived the class photograph and roster digitally; the consignor holds the physical presentation object the roster names. Separately they are interesting; together they form a verified, archival **Matched Set**.

### Why the handwritten VHPA roster holds exceptional documentary value

The handwritten VHPA roster attached below (Figure 6) holds exceptional documentary value for three reasons:

1. **Class Attrition Context:** The notation **“(orig. class mem.)”** proves the class composition changed mid-training — recording washouts, transfers, or replacements. This granular human detail is typically lost to time and never captured on official U.S. Army forms.
2. **Vietnam-Era Significance:** These 1965 Fort Wolters WOCs deployed directly into one of the highest-casualty aviation roles of the Vietnam War. A documented roster of who trained together on the TH-55A before that deployment is the exact kind of data aviation historians and families have long sought.
3. **Independent Cross-Verification:** The name **“Lancy”** appears in the top row of this handwritten roster *and* is permanently engraved on the period presentation lighter. This verifies the lighter’s inscription and proves that the object is undeniably connected to this specific historic cohort.

## 9. Later Service Context

The **Tropic Lightning News** (*Vol. 2 No. 13*, April 3, 1967) lists **WO Arthur P. Lancy**, Co. B, 25th Aviation Battalion, under the **Air Medal** section (Figure 9). This document provides **separated service-context evidence only**.

It confirms later wartime-era service affiliation for the named recipient. It does **not**:

- prove the lighter was carried in Vietnam;
- prove the lighter was used in combat;
- prove the Air Medal citation establishes object provenance;
- convert archival context into manufacture or ownership history for the lighter itself.

Service context and object evidence must be read as **independent lanes** in this packet.
