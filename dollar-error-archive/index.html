Primary Working Hypotheses

Second-Print Insufficient Inking

The leading production-error hypothesis is that the face intaglio plate contacted the paper normally but carried too little ink, producing a pale or ghosted face while preserving plate impression.

Critical test: strong physical embossing under the weak face printing.

Insufficient Impression / Pressure Failure

If the weak portions also lack normal intaglio relief, an impression-cylinder or pressure-transfer problem becomes more plausible than simple ink starvation.

Critical test: compare physical relief in weak areas with normally printed regions.

Competing explanation: post-production chemical, environmental, or abrasive alteration remains possible until a grading service examines the note under magnification, side-lighting, ultraviolet, infrared, or comparable forensic illumination.

Grading / Auction Submission Position

The archive is intentionally written as an evidence packet rather than a sales claim. A defensible submission description for Note A is:

Series 2021 $1 Federal Reserve Note, Dallas (K), serial K46876113E. Reverse/first printing appears substantially normal and complete. Face-intaglio/second printing is extremely faint across the portrait, lettering, border, and ornamental work. Green serial numbers and Treasury seal remain strong. Suspected second-print insufficient-inking or insufficient-impression error; professional authentication requested.

Recommended authentication targets: PMG or PCGS Banknote, either directly or through a qualified auction-house grading/consignment program. Final attribution should depend on physical examination rather than photographs alone.

Evidence Standard

Observed fact: the face printing is dramatically weaker than the reverse and later overprint.

Reasonable inference: the anomaly appears correlated with the face-intaglio printing stage.

Not yet proven: whether the cause was insufficient inking, insufficient impression, another BEP production fault, or later alteration.

Required to close the question: third-party physical authentication and grading.
